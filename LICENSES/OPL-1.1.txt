                              PREAMBLE
                     (or: why this thing exists)
================================================================================

Most licenses are written by lawyers, for lawyers, about lawyers, and are read
by nobody. This one is written to be read. It is still a license. The jokes are
in the headings and the asides; the sentences that begin with "You may", "You
must", and "You shall not" mean precisely what they say and are intended to be
legally operative.

Here is the deal, in one breath: **take it, run it, fork it, ship it, sell it —
and leave the names on it, and pass the same freedom along.**

Most open source licenses are quietly monolingual and monolegal. They are
drafted against one legal tradition — usually American common law — and then
exported to a hundred countries that do not share its assumptions. This causes
real problems that real projects hit:

  * Civil-law systems (France, Germany, Japan, Brazil, and most of the world)
    recognise MORAL RIGHTS that cannot be sold or waived. A license that
    ignores them is unenforceable exactly where it matters most.
  * Common-law systems require CONSIDERATION for a contract. Civil-law systems
    do not, and treat a gratuitous grant differently. A license drafted as a
    bargain may fail as a gift, and vice versa.
  * The European Union recognises a SUI GENERIS DATABASE RIGHT that is not
    copyright at all. A pure copyright license simply does not reach it.
  * Germany, the Netherlands and others VOID any attempt to disclaim liability
    for intent or gross negligence. A blanket disclaimer is not merely
    unenforceable there — it can invalidate the surrounding clause.
  * Anti-circumvention law (WIPO Copyright Treaty Art. 11, EU InfoSoc Art. 6,
    US DMCA §1201) creates a separate legal layer that a copyright grant alone
    does not clear.

This License is drafted to survive all of that at once. It grants every right
it can reach under every system it can reach, it satisfies the requirements of
each rather than assuming one, and where a jurisdiction refuses a clause, the
clause bends instead of breaking.

The Streisand Engine License (SEL-3.3) that accompanies this Work is a
statement of philosophy. It is very good at being a statement of philosophy. It
is not good at granting copyright, granting patents, disclaiming warranties, or
surviving contact with an acquisitions lawyer, because it cheerfully says it is
not trying to be. Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 is a real license,
but it was built for prose and pictures, and Creative Commons themselves advise
against using it for software.

So this License does the boring load-bearing work. It is the structural steel.
SEL-3.3 is the flag flying off the top of the building. You want both. Buildings
need steel. Flags need buildings.

"Piracy," here, means the good kind: copying a thing because copying is what
information does when you poke it. It does not mean taking someone's name off
their work. That is not piracy. That is plagiarism, and pirates have always
known the difference.
