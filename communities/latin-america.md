# ZXV → Latin American makers & repair movements

*A presentation for the hardware hackers of the region that made "right to
repair" a way of life.*

## Who you are

From Brazil's thriving maker and free-software scene, to Argentina's engineering
community, to the region-wide culture of *reparación* — keeping hardware alive
for a decade because replacing it is not an option — Latin America has a deep,
practical open-hardware ethos. You value machines you can fix, software you can
own, and independence from any single foreign vendor's roadmap.

## Why we are here

ZXV is built for the long haul and for independence. It is a from-scratch,
freestanding OS — no Linux, no libc, no vendor blob — small enough that a local
engineer can read the *entire* system and keep it running without asking anyone.
It boots on affordable ARM64 boards, networks for real over the internet, and is
post-quantum from the first commit, so a machine deployed today is not
cryptographically obsolete in five years.

Its license stack is deliberately multi-jurisdictional — drafted to be genuinely
free under civil-law systems (which most of the region uses), not just under US
common law. Your rights under it hold in your courts, in your language.

## The specific hook

- **Runs on cheap, repairable hardware and scales up when you have it.** The
  same OS resolves an embedded profile on a salvaged SBC and a server profile on
  a donated rack — one codebase, no per-machine rebuild.
- **Sovereign by construction.** Mutual sovereignty, symmetric trust, no
  one-way surveillance — compiled into the system, matching a community that
  has learned not to depend on anyone else's infrastructure.

## The honest ask

ZXV boots on ARM64 today. It is documented — including its limits — in a way
meant to be *taught from*, which suits a region with strong university and
hackerspace networks. Boot it, translate the docs, teach from the source, and
tell us where it breaks. Everything is under a license that protects your right
to do exactly that.

> Software you can keep alive, on hardware you can keep repairing.
