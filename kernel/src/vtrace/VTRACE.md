# vtrace: pictures and films to vector

The rule from the owner: the system draws vector natively (ZXVG, `zui/zui_vec.h`),
and anything visual that arrives as pixels is turned into vector, so it scales
like everything else. A picture (PNG, JPEG, GIF, BMP and the rest of
`zx_image`) becomes ZXVG. A film (Y4M, Motion-JPEG AVI/MP4/MOV, raw RGB AVI,
animated GIF, or whatever codec a platform adapter decodes for `zui_video`)
becomes ZXVM, a vector film, and plays back at any size.

Everything here is integer only, freestanding and allocation-free. The caller
provides the work memory, and `zvt_work_size`, `zvm_writer_work_size`,
`zvm_trace_video_work` and `zvm_probe(...).work` say how much is needed. Every
reader fails closed.

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `zui_vtrace.h/.c` | one picture to ZXVG, the fidelity measures, the ZXVT provenance record |
| `zui_vtrace_video.h/.c` | a film to ZXVM (keyframes and deltas), and the player (probe, open, next frame, rewind, seek) |
| `zvt_kern.h` | the per-pixel stages, written once in the subset that is C, CUDA C++ and Metal |
| `zvt_gpu.h`, `zvt_cuda.c` | CUDA backend: NVRTC at run time, libcuda opened with dlopen, no toolkit needed to build |
| `zvt_vk.c`, `zvt_vk.comp`, `gen_zvt_vk_spv.sh` | Vulkan backend: the GLSL port of `zvt_kern.h` and its SPIR-V build; the loader opened with dlopen; builds as stubs without the Vulkan headers |
| `test_zui_vtrace.c` | the main test (87 checks; part of `src/zui/test_zui_toolkit.sh`) |
| `test_zvt_gpu.c`, `test_zvt_cuda_emu.cpp`, `test_zvt_fake_cuda.cpp`, `test_zvt_cuda_ptx.h`, `test_vtrace_gpu.sh` | the GPU proofs (see below) |
| `test/fx_vtrace.inc` | fixtures, generated by `tools/vtrace/make_fixtures.py` |
| `tools/vtrace/vtrace.c` | the command-line tool, including the `--bench` corpus driver |

## Pipeline (one picture)

1. **Flatten.** A pixel with alpha < 128 is transparent and is never drawn; every other pixel is opaque.
2. **Smooth** (optional). Edge-preserving 3x3 passes (`zk_smooth_px`).
3. **Palette.** Median cut on a 5-5-5 histogram, then k-means in full 8-bit RGB. In pixel-exact mode the picture's own colours are used, unchanged.
4. **Regions.**
   - Pixels are grouped into 4-connected regions; the edge map comes from the backend.
   - Regions smaller than `min_area` are merged into the neighbour with the nearest colour.
   - Anti-aliasing fringes are absorbed. A fringe is a region about one pixel thick whose colour lies between two neighbouring colours and that touches both along its length. Each of its pixels goes to the nearest-coloured neighbour.
   - A thin line that has the same colour on both sides is not a fringe, and it stays.
5. **Boundaries.** The crack graph of the label map is traced once into chains between junctions. Every chain is shared by exactly two regions.
6. **Curves.**
   - Corners are kept: long straight runs on both sides, sharp turns, and image corners.
   - Each crack's point is moved across the crack to the sub-pixel edge implied by the two pixels' coverage of the two colours, by at most half a pixel. Corners move to where the two sub-pixel edges meet.
   - Two [1 2 1] smoothing passes follow.
   - Then a Schneider least-squares cubic fit with Newton reparameterisation, split until every point is within the tolerance.
   - A near-straight span becomes LINE, and a cubic that is really a quadratic becomes QUAD.
7. **Layering.**
   - Regions form a forest in Prim's order, largest first. Each region's parent is an earlier, adjacent region with the longest shared border.
   - A region is drawn as the union of its subtree, so its children paint over it and their anti-aliased edges blend with the parent's colour, never with the background.
   - The first region is drawn as every opaque pixel, so no gap can show the surface.
8. **Gradients** (optional). A large region whose colour fits a plane gets a two-stop linear gradient.
9. **Emit.**
   - The output is ZXVG v1, back to front, with one NEWPATH..FILL per region.
   - Every path stays within `zui_vec`'s caps. A path over the caps has its chains refitted coarser; this applies in pixel-exact mode too, and the result is then reported honestly as APPROXIMATE.
   - If that is still not enough, `min_area` grows and transparent specks smaller than `min_area` are filled.
10. **Measure.** The ZXVG is drawn back through `zui_vec` at the source size and compared with the source.
    - PSNR is computed over RGB, in centi-dB.
    - SSIM is computed on luma, over 8x8 windows at a stride of 4, x10000.
    - EXACT is reported only when the re-render is pixel-identical (checked over two backgrounds when the source has transparency).

## Formats

**ZXVG.** The format is unchanged (`zui_vec.h`). Its reader rejects any byte after END, so ZXVG carries no metadata.

**ZXVT** is a 112-byte sidecar record bound to the ZXVG by the ZXVG's SHA-256. It holds:
- the source CID (CIDv1 raw sha2-256, as in `tripartite_fs` and `ipfs_node`);
- PSNR, SSIM, preset, size, paths and colours;
- the EXACT flag;
- a check hash.

The layout is in `zui_vtrace.h`. Nothing is ever called lossless unless it is EXACT, and the original stays addressable by its CID.

**ZXVM** is the vector film. The layout is in `zui_vtrace_video.h`:
- a 96-byte header;
- one record per frame;
- a 32-byte-per-frame table holding pts and duration in microseconds, the record offset and length, per-frame PSNR and SSIM, and key or delta;
- a SHA-256 trailer.

The records work like this:
- A keyframe carries the GOP's palette and all of its shapes.
- A delta frame carries only the shapes that are not already in the GOP's table (found by hash and then a byte comparison). Every other shape is a reference to the table plus its new start point, so a shape that is unchanged or only moved costs a few bytes.
- Keyframes come every `gop` frames (default 60), at a scene change (more than 30 % of pixels change by more than 40 in some channel, counted by the backend's diff stage), and when a GOP's table would overflow.

The player rebuilds each frame's ZXVG byte for byte. It is exactly the ZXVG the tracer wrote for that frame, and the test checks this. `zvm_open` rebuilds every frame once and runs `zui_zxvg_check` on each before it accepts the file.

## GPU

The per-pixel stages run behind `zvt_backend_t`: smoothing, palette assignment, the edge map, the fidelity sums and the scene diff. A backend that refuses a call falls back to the CPU, and the result counts the fallback. All three implementations come from one integer source, so the bits are the same by construction, and the tests check that they are.

What was proved here, with no NVIDIA GPU:
- **Vulkan, run.** On lavapipe (llvmpipe), all 288 stage runs are bit-identical across 8 sizes x 3 pictures, including grids above 256 threads and odd sizes. All 12 whole traces (3 fixtures x 4 presets) produce byte-identical ZXVG and identical scores with 0 fallbacks, and the 14-frame film produces a byte-identical ZXVM. In the bench, 48 of 48 Vulkan rows are bit-exact against the CPU.
- **CUDA, compiled and emulated, not run on a GPU.**
  - The generated source compiles to PTX for sm_75, sm_86 and sm_90 (6 entry points each) with clang and stub built-ins, with no CUDA toolkit installed.
  - The same kernels run on the CPU at `zvt_cuda.c`'s launch geometry (`test_zvt_cuda_emu`, 80 of 80 runs bit-identical).
  - `zvt_cuda.c`'s whole host path (loading, NVRTC, module and function lookup, buffers, copies, launches and arguments) was run against a stand-in libcuda/libnvrtc that executes those kernels on the CPU. Its stage runs, traces and film are identical to the CPU's.
  - The first run on real hardware is `test_zvt_gpu` on the A6000 server, with `ZVT_GPU_REQUIRE=cuda,vulkan`.
- **Metal: not written.** `zvt_kern.h` keeps to the subset Metal accepts, but there is no `zvt_metal.m` yet.

Still on the CPU: median cut and the k-means averaging, labelling, merging, chains, curve fitting, layering, emitting and the `zui_vec` re-render. A GPU therefore speeds up only the per-pixel share. The lavapipe "speed-ups" in the bench summary are CPU against CPU and mean nothing.

## Commands

From `kernel/`:

```sh
sh src/zui/test_zui_toolkit.sh                 # includes test_zui_vtrace
sh src/vtrace/test_vtrace_gpu.sh               # all GPU proofs; ZXV_TMP=dir to keep the binaries
ZVT_GPU_REQUIRE=cuda,vulkan sh src/vtrace/test_vtrace_gpu.sh   # on the GPU server
python3 ../tools/vtrace/make_fixtures.py [--corpus DIR]        # fixtures (and a starting corpus)
```

To build the CLI, with Vulkan included when the SPIR-V header exists:

```sh
sh src/vtrace/gen_zvt_vk_spv.sh /tmp/zvt_vk_spv.h
gcc -std=c11 -O2 -DTEST_HOST -Iinclude -Isrc/zui -Isrc/font -Isrc/media -Isrc/robin_debanks -Isrc/vtrace \
  -Isrc/emu -Isrc/lpres -Isrc/event_space -Isrc/modbind -Isrc/surplus -DZVT_VK_SPV_H='"/tmp/zvt_vk_spv.h"' \

  src/vtrace/zvt_vk.c src/robin_debanks/sha256.c src/zui/zui_vec.c src/zui/zui_canvas.c src/media/zx_image.c \
  src/media/zui_png.c src/media/zui_jpeg.c src/media/zx_inflate.c src/media/zui_video.c src/font/truetype.c \
  src/zui/zui_capture.c src/zui/zui_wm.c src/zui/zui_dock.c src/zui/zui_motion.c src/zui/zui_layout.c \
  src/zui/zui_progress.c -ldl -o vtrace
```

To use it:

```sh
./vtrace --preset icon --render out.ppm --scale 4 logo.png -o logo   # logo.zxvg, logo.zxvt, a 4x render
./vtrace --backend auto film.avi                                      # film.zxvm
./vtrace --bench CORPUS --presets all --backends all --jsonl rows.jsonl --summary summary.json
```

Each `--bench` row has these fields:
- `file`, `kind` (image or video), `cid`, `backend`, `preset`;
- `ms`, `bytes`;
- `psnr_cdb` (999999 means identical; for a film, the average with each frame capped at 9999), `ssim_x10000` (for a film, the average);
- `exact`, `bit_exact_vs_cpu` (null for the CPU row);
- plus `w`, `h`, `frames`, `paths` or `keyframes`, `fallbacks`, and `error` with `code` when a run failed.

The summary gives, per backend and preset: runs, ok, failed, total ms, total bytes, mean scores, exact count, bit-exact count and speed-up against the CPU. The exit status is 1 if any run failed.

## Limits (honest)

**What the vector form leaves out**
- Photographs, film, fine texture, noise and grain are represented by flat regions plus a few linear gradients. The scores in the tables are what they are.
- Textured art is expensive. A 3888² logo with fine text texture became a 10.3 MB ZXVG (33.9 dB) and took 22.6 s on one core.
- Detail below `min_area` is merged away.
- A light line one or two pixels wide between dark outlines can lose a pixel of width.
- Downscaled line art with sub-pixel lines loses some line segments. The 256² brand icon scores 23.55 dB, against 40 dB at 1024².
- Partial alpha is reduced to opaque or transparent.
- Strokes, arcs and radial gradients are never emitted; only fills are.
- The palette has at most 256 colours. Pixel-exact output is possible only for flat pictures within that.

**Films**
- Reuse is whole shapes only. A shape whose outline changed anywhere is stored again.
- There is no motion search beyond exact shape matches.
- There is no temporal smoothing, so a noisy still region can retrace differently from frame to frame.
- There is no audio.
- Gradients are off in films.

**Other limits**
- Size is limited to `ZVT_MAX_DIM` = 8192 per side and 16 M pixels. The work memory is about 200 B per pixel.
- On i386 the code needs the compiler's 64-bit division helpers (`__divdi3`, `__udivdi3`). On x86-64 and aarch64 freestanding builds it needs nothing outside the kernel.
- GPU: see above. CUDA has not yet run on an NVIDIA device, and Metal is not written.
